# The design direction

**Chosen: Late Edition.** Decided by four judges scoring on separate criteria, each having
read all three directions in full from disk, and a director required to state the case
against its own choice.

| Direction | Originality | Craft | Fit | Endurance | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Late Edition** | 7 | 9 | 9 | 8 | **66** |
| **TEST CARD** | 9 | 5 | 8 | 9 | **62** |
| **Bias Light** | 8 | 7 | 7 | 5 | **54** |

This replaces a first round whose judges were handed a truncated copy and scored
essentially one direction — see `design/directions/README.md`. The three directions
themselves are unchanged; only the comparison is new.

## Are the three genuinely different?

Genuinely different at the thesis and the information architecture; near-identical at the surface. Both halves of that are findings about the process rather than about the three teams.

What is genuinely different is not stylistic, it is structural, and it produces different apps. Late Edition has no record screen at all. TEST CARD makes the record a tab and demotes Watchlist into it. Bias Light has neither and spends the top 44% of the home screen on one 16:9 still. Those are three different answers to "what is this app a picture of" — a page you mark up, a room you sit in, an archive you keep — and they fork the navigation, not just the palette. The refusals differ genuinely too, and refusals are where a direction says what it is: Late Edition refuses the poster wall on the argument that the library is mostly unmatched, Bias Light builds one; Bias Light refuses unwatched stills and any public score, and neither other document mentions spoilers or crowd scores at all. And the signatures sit at three genuinely different points in the loop: Late Edition's is the action, Bias Light's is the moment after the action, TEST CARD's is the accumulated consequence of every action. That is a real spread and it was worth commissioning three.

But roughly seventy per cent of the pixels are the same, and the convergence list is long enough to be structural rather than coincidental. All three kill the rounded card for hairline rules and flat surfaces. All three refuse blur, glass, gradient and glow. All three adopt a condensed tracked-caps face for section heads and metadata — two of them independently pick the identical font file, Archivo Narrow. All three set every number in tabular mono. All three turn the missing poster into a designed typographic object with the title set large and prominent instead of 10pt centred grey; VHS spine, plate and Penguin title card are one insight in three costumes. All three promote Discover's repeated reason to a single section head — verbatim the same fix. All three raise the 9-10pt labels to 11-12pt, rebuild the Watching Mode control row into equal cells that reflow at 200%, replace the hamburger, give the active tab a second channel, and make the import progress determinate with the count beside it. And all three — not two, as the judges had it — independently invented reaction density as a persistent shape under the stream: Bias Light's heat rule, TEST CARD's histogram, and Late Edition's 6pt tick marks on a timeline beneath each reaction. Three independent arrivals is about as strong as design evidence gets; build it.

The tell for why is theme.ts. All three open dark-first and justify it with a sentence that is nearly a quotation of the comment already in /home/user/Reviewies/mobile/src/ui/theme.ts — "this app gets used in a dark room with the television on." The brief, OBSERVED.md, the code comments and fourteen screenshots documented the problems so specifically that the *diagnosis* was over-determined, and once the diagnosis is fixed, the remedial layer follows almost mechanically. Independence bought three theses; it did not buy three visual languages, because there was only one honest visual answer to the evidence.

The sharpest proof that they really were written independently is the errors they share. All three claim the remote drawer has no affordance whatsoever; all three are wrong — RemoteDrawer.tsx:113-124 ships a 24pt tab with a proper accessible label — and all three then replace it with a handle narrower than the one they say does not exist, against the 44pt standard every one of them cites. Three writers do not converge on the same wrong premise and the same regression by copying; they converge on it by reading the same screenshot, seeing a drawer they could not find, and never opening the file. That is a process note worth more than the ranking: the next round should require every claim about the current build to name the file it was read from, because the one thing three independent readings did not protect against was a shared assumption that nobody checked.

## The comparison

Read as objects rather than as scores, the three are a page, a room, and an archive. LATE EDITION is a printed artefact you mark up: full-bleed rows on hairlines, tabular mono figures, a section rule running to the edge, and a completion gesture — the biro through the listing — that compounds into a season struck top to bottom and a year that reads as a marked-up almanac. Its bet is that the app is a record of attention already spent, so the correct materials are the ones that recorded it before software did, and its most consequential decision is to remove the dependency on artwork entirely rather than design a nicer hole for it. BIAS LIGHT is a dark room you sit in: matte letterbox bands, artwork as the only source of light, true black in Watching Mode for a stated physical reason, and a signature that is not a control at all but a moment — the hard cut, the 400ms of nothing, the title card asking what you thought. Its bet is that the product's real subject is the act of watching and the sentence you say afterwards, and its best work is physical reasoning nobody else did (auto-dim on a sofa, no stills for unwatched episodes, no crowd score anywhere) sitting on top of a layout that is a poster wall. TEST CARD is the archive made visible: ten years of watches as one band of coloured bars capping every screen, scrubbable with the thumb like a transport, with one hairline slot always open at the right end waiting for tonight. Its bet is that the record is the product and the tracker is only the instrument you read it with, and it is the only one that noticed the record currently appears exactly once in the whole build, on the seventh screen, under a paragraph about local-first storage. The page is the most buildable and the least surprising; the room is the most humane and the least dense; the archive has the only object in the three documents I cannot name a precedent for, and stakes everything on data the importer does not produce.

## The case for Late Edition

It is the only direction whose identity survives the state this product is entered in. Reviewies' entire acquisition story is "import a decade out of a dead app", and I verified in /home/user/Reviewies/core/src/data/diocache.ts what that import actually deposits: episode watches with no ratings at all (the single `ratings.push` at line 142 sits inside the films loop; the television loop at 171-216 pushes only `watches`), and titles with `fetchedAt: 0` and no artwork until matching runs. Bias Light's ambient signature is sampled off artwork, so on day one there is nothing to sample. TEST CARD's strip is keyed to rating, so on day one it is 264 flat grey ticks at #5A616D — which I measured at 2.62:1 on the very surface its own document places unrated cards on. Late Edition is the only direction that treats the absent asset as the primary case: the hand-labelled tape spine is stated as the *primary* treatment for an unmatched title, not the fallback, with the name in primary text colour instead of the 10pt centred grey that /home/user/Reviewies/mobile/src/ui/Poster.tsx renders today and 05-discover.png shows printing the title twice, once illegibly. A shelf of hand-labelled tapes is a truthful and flattering picture of exactly what that library is. The direction gets better the worse your data is.

Second, it is the only one that did the arithmetic. I re-ran all 31 published ratios through the `contrast()` function in /home/user/Reviewies/the palette guard then in app/src/ui/ and every one is exact to two decimals, including the awkward ones — tertiary #8A8079 holding 4.51 on the *raised* ground, light label yellow at 4.01 on newsprint and 4.48 carrying #FFFDF8. It is the only direction that would survive its own proposed extension of that guard, and the only one that proposes a writable extension (every text token against every ground it can land on, every label >=3:1, amber never a text token in light mode) rather than admiring the guard from a distance. TEST CARD's numbers were estimated and three of them fail on the most common state in the app.

Third, its signature is the loop rather than something attached to the loop. The cross-off *is* mark-watched: ring, 220ms stroke, colour step, haptic, undo. It fires on the highest-frequency action in the product every single time, forever, and leaves a persistent artefact rather than a one-off animation. Bias Light's signature arrives pre-rationed to the first mark of a sitting; TEST CARD's is inert until you drag it. And Late Edition's needs no new rendering technology at all — an Animated width on a 1pt View with useNativeDriver, no SVG, no Skia, no gesture handler, none of which are installed. It is the only signature in the three documents that cannot fail to ship.

Fourth, its four-state poster slot is the highest-leverage element anyone proposed: artwork / spine / test-card-at-rest / NO SIGNAL hatch, one accessible-label shape across all four. It appears on every screen, costs Views and borders, and it is the only design in the set that gives "could not look" a distinct *visual* instead of folding it into "nothing there" — closing the one place the standard that /home/user/Reviewies/core/src/copy.ts keeps scrupulously in prose is quietly broken in pixels.

Fifth, on the thing OBSERVED.md actually found — "the screens are mostly empty below the fold" — it is the only direction that attacks density as an information-design problem rather than by enlarging the pictures. Rules instead of card fills, sectioned Up Next, Discover's repeated reason promoted to one section rule, More as a contents page that fits without scrolling. Mono tabular figures are justified functionally: predictable column widths are what stop S02E07 wrapping at 200% text, and percentage-width control cells never cross the viewport edge, so it passes the `scaled()` gate in /home/user/Reviewies/mobile/smoke.mjs largely for free.

## The case against it, stated by the director who chose it

Its signature is a strikethrough, and its register is the most-taken answer to "don't look like Netflix." Crossing an item off with an animated stroke has shipped in every to-do app since 2012; pencil in dark and biro in light is charm, not invention. The delivered aesthetic — newsprint ground, condensed caps, rules to the edge, mono figures, no glass — is the standard editorial answer, and the strongest evidence for that is that TEST CARD independently arrived at three-quarters of the same typographic system from a completely different starting point. Nobody will describe this app to a friend by its style. They will describe TEST CARD's empty slot, which is the one idea in the three documents with no precedent I can name: a reward that is a true fact about you, cannot be gamed, does not nag, and appreciates the longer you keep the app — which is the "left for simplicity, stayed for the record" thesis rendered as a shape instead of argued in prose. Choosing Late Edition is choosing the available register over the genuinely new object, and I am doing it with my eyes open.

The concrete cost is a hole, not a preference: Late Edition has no record screen. At the 3,000th row, "where is the thing I watched in 2019" is answered by a horizontal strip inside Search and by Stats, which is a partial answer where TEST CARD has a structural one — and TEST CARD is right that nothing in the current tab bar leads to ten years of history at all. Its Watchlist-as-shelf also degrades badly at 900 saved titles into 113 rows of near-identical bands with rotated micro-type, and its App Store screenshot is the weakest of the three by construction, because it explicitly refuses the poster wall. Its anime answer is a browse chip and a Discover section and nothing else — no CJK face named, in a product that calls anime first-class. And "newsprint tracker" has a moment attached to it in a way that broadcast-plant plainness does not, so it is marginally the more datable of the two plain registers. I am mitigating the hole by grafting rather than by pretending it is not there; the originality gap I am simply accepting, and paying down with TEST CARD's empty slot at a fraction of its cost.

## What is grafted in from the other two

TEN GRAFTS. Each names what it displaces, because a graft that displaces nothing is decoration.

1. LIBRARY BECOMES A TAB; WATCHLIST DEMOTES INTO IT AS A SEGMENT (from TEST CARD). Displaces Late Edition's Watchlist tab, and with it the shelf as a whole screen — the shelf survives as the WATCHLIST segment, not as one of five tabs. This closes Late Edition's single largest hole. TEST CARD's justification is correct and nobody else made it: nothing in the current bar leads to the record at all, and 07-more.png shows it buried on the seventh screen under a paragraph about local-first storage. Trading a filter for the record is the right swap.

2. THE YEAR SPINE, NOT THE STRIP (from TEST CARD, cut down). One row per year — `2019 — 412 EPISODES · 21 DAYS` in Plex Mono, each row twelve month-blocks — at the top of Library and on More. This displaces TEST CARD's 28pt band capping every screen. I take the identity payoff at O(years) instead of O(watches): no Skia, no thousands of shadow-tree nodes, no fixed-height chrome tax on a product whose diagnosed disease is empty screens, no unstated 200% behaviour. Decisively, it reads correctly with zero ratings, because a year's row is made of *counts* — which the import does produce — where the strip is made of ratings, which for television it provably does not. It also lands natively in Late Edition's own idiom: its Stats treatment is already "twelve month-blocks of struck rules."

3. THE EMPTY SLOT (from TEST CARD — the best single idea in the set). The current month's block on the year spine carries one hairline-outlined open cell waiting for tonight; marking an episode strikes into it. Displaces nothing; it costs one View. This is the whole reward system and it makes the highest-frequency action in the product grow a visible object every time.

4. `working` AMBER GETS EXACTLY ONE JOB (from TEST CARD). Amber means still-reading, 3-of-12, searching, saved-but-not-delivered, and nothing else. This displaces Late Edition's plan to make Highlighter Amber the primary action colour — a real cost, paid deliberately. Primary action goes to TEST CARD's solid near-white/near-black pill instead, which I measured at 17.17:1 either way. Late Edition correctly diagnoses #ff5c7a doing six jobs across 05/08/09/13/14 and then puts two meanings on one hue itself. The cross-off ring fill stays amber only because it marks *completion* rather than availability; if that reads as a conflict in build, the ring goes to the label-set green and amber stays purely unsettled. This is the graft I am least sure of and the first thing I want tested on a device.

5. THE HEAT RULE (from Bias Light). Every reaction that drifts past leaves a 1pt amber tick on a rule at the stream band's bottom edge, until the rule is a map of where everyone reacted. Displaces TEST CARD's full-episode crowd histogram, which draws the density of everyone who ever watched *up front* — 12-account.png says sync is not configured and copy.ts's `people.withoutAccount` says shared reactions need it, so that hero is blank for the default user, and TEST CARD never designs that state. The heat rule accumulates only what has actually passed, so it starts empty honestly and fills, and `addReaction` writes locally, so it works signed-out on a rewatch. Late Edition already proposed per-reaction ticks on a timeline; this is the same object, better argued. Keep Late Edition's empty line — NO REACTIONS RECORDED, YOURS WILL BE THE FIRST — which is the best empty-state sentence in the three documents.

6. THE SPOILER RULE (from Bias Light). No stills and no episode names for unwatched episodes; the hero uses series art or the spine, never the unwatched episode's own frame. Displaces nothing in Late Edition, which never addressed spoilers at all — a genuine hole in a tracker. It is nearly free here, because Late Edition already refuses the 16:9 hero that made this expensive for Bias Light.

7. DETERMINISTIC HASH FOR THE SPINE'S BAND COLOUR, CLAMPED IN OKLCH (from Bias Light). Displaces Late Edition's unstated method for picking which of its eight label colours a spine gets. The same show is always the same colour, so a shelf of unmatched titles becomes navigable by memory instead of being 113 arbitrary bands. Note this clamp is cheap where Bias Light's was not: it runs on a hash, not on sampled pixels — no native colour module, no image decode, and not dead on day one.

8. IBM PLEX SANS JP (from TEST CARD). Displaces Late Edition's silence on Japanese and Archivo Narrow's claim on CJK titles specifically. Anime is first-class and Late Edition gave it a browse chip. Carrying the caveat into the build rather than hiding it: RN's `fontFamily` takes one string with no fallback list, so a mixed Latin/CJK title needs per-string script detection at the call site — small, real work that TEST CARD's document assumes away.

9. AUTO-DIM IN WATCHING MODE, AT 75% NOT 55% (from Bias Light). Displaces nothing; Late Edition has no dim, and Bias Light's physical argument — the phone stops being a lit rectangle in your peripheral vision — is the best sofa reasoning anyone did. The number changes because I ran it: at 55% over #000000, muted composites to 2.93 and coral to 2.98. At 75% muted is 4.73. Dim to 75%, or dim only the primary-text elements.

10. THE PROMPT AFTER MARKING, AS AN INLINE CARD (from Bias Light, stripped of its ceremony). Six emoji, a focused field, six-second self-dismissal, in the row you just crossed off. Displaces Bias Light's Cut to Black and its 120ms flicker, both refused outright — that is atmosphere billed at the rate of the core loop, on the one action that most needs to feel instant, in a dark room. The insight underneath is right and it is the best thing in that document: the credits are where people talk, and marking watched currently dead-ends. Correction to Bias Light's framing, which matters for the build: 13-data.png already ships "Open the conversation after marking watched — How TV Time worked. Needs an account," toggled on. This is not a discovered hole, it is a declared one with no design — but it does mean the toggle's subtitle has to change, since this writes a local review and needs no account. That is a copy change, and copy has one owner.

## What is refused from the winner

EIGHT REFUSALS.

1. AMBER AS THE PRIMARY ACTION COLOUR. Refused, per graft 4. Late Edition correctly diagnoses one accent doing six jobs in the current build and then asks one hue to mean both "press this" and "still working", in a product whose non-negotiable standard is that nothing / could-not-look / still-working stay distinct. Primary action becomes the near-white pill; amber is unsettled states and the completion ring, and if those two conflict on a device, amber is unsettled states only.

2. "ARCHIVO EXPANDED 700" AS A FACE, AND THE ECONOMY IT IS SOLD ON. Refused. Archivo Expanded is a named instance of the variable Archivo, not a static Google Fonts family, and React Native cannot set variable axes — there is no `fontVariationSettings` and `fontFamily` resolves to one file. The direction's claim, "one superfamily with real width axes, so the app has range without a second family", is false on this platform: each width is a separate file that must be instanced out with fonttools in a build step this repo does not have. Mastheads use Archivo Narrow 700 tracked out, or the instance gets generated and checked in — but the claim is load-bearing and it is wrong. Three related traps it does not name: once `fontFamily` is set, `fontWeight` is ignored on Android and faux-synthesised on iOS, so Narrow 600 and 700 must be two loaded families and Newsreader Italic its own family rather than `fontStyle: 'italic'`; `letterSpacing` in RN is points, not em, so every `+0.12em` must be computed from the scaled size at each call site or the tracked caps come apart at 200%; and `expo-font` is not in /home/user/Reviewies/mobile/package.json, so seven files is seven files plus a dependency.

3. THE 34pt SHELF SPINE. Refused at that width. It is a drag-and-tap target below the 44pt floor, edge-to-edge with its neighbours, and hitSlop cannot rescue it — /home/user/Reviewies/mobile/src/ui/theme.ts already explains why: "two hitSlop targets next to each other overlap, and nothing can measure them." Spines go to 44pt minimum, about seven per row instead of eight, or the shelf becomes presentation with a 44pt row-level target and the LIST view is the interactive path. (The same refusal, applied outward: all three directions call the remote drawer handle "no affordance whatsoever" and all three replace the existing 24pt labelled tab at RemoteDrawer.tsx:113-124 with something narrower — 12pt, 3pt, 4pt. Whichever handle ships is 44pt.)

4. THE CLAIM THAT NOTHING SURFACES `unwatchLatest` WHERE THE ERROR HAPPENS. Refused as a premise. /home/user/Reviewies/mobile/app/(tabs)/index.tsx:82 and /home/user/Reviewies/app/src/screens/UpNext.tsx:177 already wire Undo to the `justMarked` copy on Up Next. The 6-second chip at the ring you mis-tapped is a real improvement in proximity and I want it — but it refines a shipped capability, and a direction that mis-describes what it is replacing will mis-price it.

5. THE ASIDE THAT TODAY'S MUTED GREY "DOES NOT HOLD AA CONSISTENTLY" ON THE RAISED GROUND. Refused: #9a9ab0 measures 5.91 on #1f1f2b and 6.59 on #15151d. It is the one wrong number in thirty-one, and it is the one that flatters the new palette. In a document whose authority rests on having run the function, that is worth striking rather than quietly keeping.

6. THE MONO TRANSMISSION LINE AT FULL WIDTH. `264 WATCHES · 12 TRACKED · 5.5 DAYS` is about thirty characters of the widest-per-character face in the app; at 26pt it needs roughly 460pt on a 390pt frame, and at 200% it wraps or clips. It is not a control, so the `scaled()` gate in /home/user/Reviewies/mobile/smoke.mjs will not catch it — which makes it worse, not better. It stacks, or it drops a clause by rule.

7. "NO POSTER WALL" AS AN ABSOLUTE LAW. Kept as the default, refused as a law. Library at 3-up with rating bars is a poster grid, and it is the right instrument in the one place the task is recognition across 900 titles rather than reading. The rule that survives is "browsing is reading, not shopping"; the rule that does not is one that forbids a grid on the record screen the direction did not have.

8. ANIME AS A BROWSE CHIP. Refused as sufficient, per graft 8. For a product where anime is first-class, one chip and one Discover section is not an answer, and a mixed library falling through to a system CJK face that does not match Archivo Narrow is the exact defect TEST CARD names and Late Edition does not see.

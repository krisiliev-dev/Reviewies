# TEST CARD

One of three visual directions commissioned for Reviewies, each generated from a
different starting point and written without sight of the others.

## The idea

Television's oldest image is the one it shows when nothing is on: the test card, the colour bars, the pattern engineers transmitted to prove the signal was true. This app holds ten years of somebody's signal and currently hides it — the only place the record appears in the whole build is a grey 3-up stat block ("Watches 264 / Tracked 12 / 5.5 days watched") sitting on the seventh screen, under More, below a paragraph about local-first storage. Every other screen leads with a 34pt title over 900px of void. TEST CARD inverts that. Your history becomes the app's wallpaper and its primary navigation object: every episode you have ever watched is one thin vertical bar, coloured and sized by what you thought of it, and those bars are stacked into a strip that caps every screen like a station ident. It is a test card generated by attention that nobody else has. Everything around it is broadcast-plant plainness — hairline rules, flat surfaces, monospaced tabular figures, condensed listings-magazine titles, no decoration that is not a fact. The screen name shrinks to a corner slug; the show you are about to watch gets the display size. The record is the product; the tracker is the instrument you read it with.

## The signature move

THE STRIP — and scrubbing your own life with your thumb.

A 28pt band caps every screen. One vertical bar per watch, oldest left, tonight right; height and colour by rating; unrated watches a flat grey tick. 264 watches is 264 bars. Ten years is a picture of your taste that fits above the fold and that nobody else has.

It is not decoration, it is a control. Drag your thumb along it and you scrub your own history: the bar under your thumb blooms into a small card — poster or title-card, S02E07, the date in mono, your star line, the first clause of what you wrote. Let go and it snaps back. The gesture is deliberately the one a video player uses, because that is the joke and the point: you are scrubbing memory instead of runtime. Pinch to zoom from week to decade. VoiceOver gets it as an adjustable slider — swipe up/down moves a watch at a time, and the value announcement is the same sentence the bloom card shows, so the feature is not sighted-only.

The half that people will actually describe to a friend: at the far right there is always ONE EMPTY SLOT, one bar wide, hairline outline, waiting for tonight. The record is visibly unfinished. When you mark an episode watched, a bar strikes up into that slot and the whole strip shifts left by one. Every mark grows the object. That is the entire reward system — no badges, no streak flames, no confetti — and it is more satisfying than any of them because it is true and it accumulates.

Up Next shows the last 90 days. Library shows all of it. The You screen shows a year per row, ten rows, and that stack is your profile picture.

## Palette

The current palette is one accent doing six jobs. In screenshots #ff5c7a is simultaneously the active tab, the primary button, the selected chip, the star fill, the "Scan Wi-Fi" link, the OK key on the remote, and the checkmark on the notification choice. Nothing is distinguishable from anything. TEST CARD splits colour by MEANING and gives brightness the job of signalling action.

DARK (default — the app is used in a dark room with the TV on)
  ink        #0B0C0E   page. A CRT black, marginally cooler than today's #0b0b0f
  surface    #14161A   cards, rows, the pad buttons
  surface2   #1D2026   pressed, inset wells, unrated poster cards
  edge       #2A2E36   1px hairlines. Currently the tab-bar rule is near-invisible; this is a real line
  text       #F4F5F7   ≈18:1 on ink
  muted      #A2A8B4   ≈8.1:1 on ink (today's #9a9ab0 is ≈7:1 — fine, this is a touch clearer and cooler)
  faint      #6E7580   ≈3.9:1 — NON-TEXT ONLY: hairline emphasis, disabled glyph strokes, histogram fill

LIGHT (the log book — reading ten years of record in daylight is a reading task and paper wins)
  paper      #F7F6F3   warm, not white
  surface    #FFFFFF
  surface2   #EDEBE6
  edge       #DAD7D0
  text       #16181C   ≈16:1 on paper
  muted      #5B6068   ≈5.7:1 on paper
  faint      #8B9098   non-text only

THE RATING RAMP — colour means one thing in this app and this is it. Rating, and nothing else.
  Dark set (marks on ink, all ≥3:1 for WCAG 1.4.11 non-text):
    unrated  #5A616D    1★ #3D6FD6 (≈4.1:1)   2★ #2BA0B4 (≈6.3:1)
    3★ #55A64E (≈6.4:1)   4★ #D9A521 (≈8.6:1)   5★ #E14B94 (≈5.3:1)
  Light set (darkened — the dark set drops to 2.0–2.7:1 on paper and must not be reused):
    unrated  #A9A69E    1★ #2F55A8   2★ #17707E   3★ #3A7433   4★ #8E6605   5★ #B32A6B
    all ≥4.5:1 on paper, so in light theme these can also carry text
  Text-safe variants for dark, when a rating hue must set a word:
    #7FA0F0 · #63C6D6 · #86CE80 · #F0C557 · #F587BA — all ≥6:1 on ink
  Cold-to-warm is the ordering because it is the one people read as indifference-to-love without a key. 5★ magenta keeps a rope back to today's pink so this is an evolution, not amnesia.

  CRITICAL: colour is never the only encoding. Every bar in the strip encodes rating by HEIGHT as well as hue — 1★ is 25% of the band, 5★ is 100%, unrated is a flat 2px baseline tick. The strip is fully readable in greyscale, at any CVD, and by anyone who never learns the key. That is what makes a seven-colour system defensible in an accessibility-serious codebase.

SEMANTIC COLOURS — each owns exactly one job, which is the fix for the accent overload
  action     dark: #F2F0EA solid pill, ink text · light: #16181C solid pill, paper text
             Primary buttons stop being pink. Brightness = "this is the thing to press", ≈17:1 either way.
  here       #E14B94   selection, the active tab's tally rule, your own reactions in the histogram. Never a button fill.
  working    #D9A521   and ONLY this: "still reading", "3 of 12", "searching", "saved but not delivered". The house rule about unsettled states finally gets a colour of its own.
  trouble    #E8433F   error, "I could not look", destructive. Appears nowhere decorative. #E8433F on ink ≈4.9:1.
  Secondary buttons: transparent with a 1px `edge` border and `text` label — currently "Watchlist" and "Later" are grey-on-grey pills that read as disabled.

## Typography

Three families, three jobs. Two are on Google Fonts, all three have a system fallback so the app still ships if the bundle fails.

  DISPLAY — Archivo Condensed (fallback: SF Compact Display)
    Titles of shows, screen heroes, card names, poster title-cards. Condensed is not styling here, it is load-bearing: in 05-discover, "Hollow Crown Saga" and "Kitchen Nightshift" already crowd their line at 390pt, and an anime library is full of titles twice that length. Condensed buys ~22% more characters per line and reads like a listings magazine — Radio Times, TV Guide — which is exactly the register.
  TEXT — IBM Plex Sans (fallback: SF Pro Text), with IBM Plex Sans JP shipped for CJK
    Every sentence, every review, every honest empty state. Plex JP matters: anime is a first-class concern and Japanese titles currently fall through to a system face that does not match the Latin, so a mixed library looks typeset by two people.
  FIGURES — IBM Plex Mono (fallback: SF Mono)
    Everything countable, always tabular: 264, 5.5d, S02E07, 44m, 00:14:07, 2019, dates, the 3-of-12 counter, the strip's axis. This is where the personality lives. The record is made of numbers and this direction refuses to set them in the same face as the prose.

SCALE (all in scalable units; every line survives 200% Dynamic Type — verified against the two places the code already documents failures, the Watching Mode control row and the density chips)
  slug        Plex Mono 500 / 12 / 16 / +0.08em / UPPERCASE / muted
              Screen idents and section heads. "TELL ME ABOUT" and "NEXT TWO MONTHS" on 09-calendar already do this by accident — formalise it and demote "Up Next", "Discover", "Watchlist" into it.
  title-xl    Archivo Cond 700 / 30 / 32     the one show on Up Next; a title page's name
  title-l     Archivo Cond 700 / 22 / 26     card titles
  title-m     Archivo Cond 600 / 17 / 22     list rows
  body        Plex Sans 400 / 16 / 24        sentences, reviews
  body-s      Plex Sans 400 / 14 / 20        secondary sentences, settings rows
  figure-xl   Plex Mono 500 / 34 / 36 tnum   "264", "5.5d", "0m"
  figure      Plex Mono 400 / 13 / 18 tnum   S02E07 · 44m · 12 Aug · 00:14:07
  micro       Plex Sans 500 / 11 / 14        chip labels, emoji-pad labels (up from the current 9pt, which is unreadable in a dark room and is the single worst type decision in the build)

  Two header systems currently coexist — 01/02/03/05/06/07 use ~34pt heavy; 08/09/10/14 use ~26pt regular. That is not a style choice, it is drift. One system: slug for the screen, title-xl for the content.
  Personality carrier: the mono figures and the tracked-caps slugs. A review's first sentence is pulled out as a title-l pull-quote in Archivo Cond — the one place the app raises its voice, and it raises it for the user's own words.

## The poster slot

This is the highest-leverage change in the review, because the evidence shows what a real import looks like: `Poster.tsx` renders a 64×96 `surface2` rounded rectangle with the title at 10pt `muted`, three lines, centred — and the file's own comment admits "imported history is full of titles that never matched a provider". A ten-year TV Time export will be mostly these. Today they read as broken images. In this direction they read as a designed shelf.

FRAME (all posters, image or not)
  2:3, SQUARE CORNERS, 1px `edge` hairline, plus a second 1px rule inset 3px — a slide mount. Posters lose their radius; cards and buttons keep theirs. That contrast becomes a system rule: images are rectangles, controls are rounded. The double rule is what makes real artwork and title-cards sit together without one looking like a failure of the other.
  A 3px bar along the bottom edge in the rating colour. Unrated: no bar. This is how a poster grid becomes readable as taste at a glance, and it works in the Library grid at 3-up where nothing else would.

NO POSTER → TITLE CARD, not a grey box
  Ground: the rating hue at 12% over `surface`; `surface2` if unrated.
  Top-left: mono slug, `2021 · SERIES` — facts the current fallback throws away.
  Body: the title in Archivo Condensed 700, LEFT-aligned, ragged right, set as large as fits in three lines (auto-shrink 22 → 13, never below 13). Left-aligned and large is the whole difference between a typographic poster and an apology; 10pt centred grey is the apology.
  Bottom: the rating bar, as above.
  It is a Penguin spine. A screen of them looks intentional.

THE THREE STATES, at the image level — nothing in the build currently distinguishes these, which is the one place the codebase's own honesty standard is quietly broken
  no poster        → title card, permanent, no spinner, accessible name "<Title>, no artwork"
  still loading    → the empty frame with a 1px `working`-amber sweep across the inner rule; reduced motion: static 25% `edge` fill, no movement
  could not look   → title card + a 6pt amber corner tick, accessible name ending "artwork unavailable offline"
  Never a pulsing grey rectangle. A shimmer that means empty, loading and broken at once is exactly the ambiguity this codebase spent §6.57 and §6.79 eliminating in text.

SIZES: 64×96 in rows, 108×162 in the Library 3-up grid, 132×198 in Discover shelves, and a full-bleed 16:9 still on the Up Next hero with the title card cropped to letterbox if there is no still.

## The screens

TAB BAR
  Now: five tabs with four unrelated icon idioms — a filled vector triangle, a ~14pt magnifier glyph, a sparkle, a 24pt HAMBURGER standing for Watchlist, and an ellipsis. The hamburger means "menu" everywhere else on earth. The sparkle now reads as "AI". Active state is pink icon + pink label and nothing else.
  Becomes: UP NEXT · LIBRARY · SEARCH · DISCOVER · YOU. One icon idiom — 1.5pt stroke, 24pt box, square-cut terminals, drawn from plant objects: play-triangle-in-a-frame, a three-bar stack (the strip itself), magnifier, an antenna arc, and a mini strip forming a monogram. Active = label `muted`→`text`, weight 400→600, plus a 2px `here`-magenta rule ON TOP of the tab — a tally light. Three cues, not one. Tab bar sits on `ink` with a real `edge` top rule.
  Watchlist leaves the bar and becomes the first segment inside Library. Justification: nothing in the current tab bar leads to the ten years of history at all, and Reddit's loudest complaint about the survivors was that getting to your own shows is a crapshoot. Trading a filter for the record is the right swap.

1 · UP NEXT — now: "Up Next" at 34pt, two grey sentences, one pink pill, then 900px of black. On the seeded 264-watch library it is still empty.
  Becomes: strip (last 90 days) → HERO: full-bleed 16:9 still or letterboxed title card, `S02E07 · 44m · AIRED 12 AUG` in mono over it, episode name in title-xl, and two actions — a full-width `action` pill MARK WATCHED and an outlined WATCH that opens Watching Mode. Below: a horizontal shelf of the other shows waiting, poster + mono `S2E7` badge. Below that a `SINCE YOU LAST OPENED` slug listing what newly aired.
  The importing state stays honest but moves INSIDE the hero frame: amber `STILL READING` slug, the existing sentence in body, and a determinate 3-of-12 bar. Right now "Matching… 3 of 12, 0 matched so far" is grey body text with no geometry — the one number the person cares about has no shape.

2 · LIBRARY — does not exist today. The record screen.
  Full strip, pinch-zoomable decade↔week. Under it a YEAR SPINE: one row per year, each a mini-strip, `2019 — 412 EPISODES · 21 DAYS` in mono. Tap a year, it expands to months. Segments: ALL · WATCHING · WATCHLIST · FILMS · ANIME. Then a 3-up poster grid with rating bars. Sort by date watched, rating, or runtime. This is the screen that makes the app an identity rather than a queue.

3 · SEARCH — now: a bare outlined field on pure black. No history, no local results, no distinction between "nothing found" and "offline".
  Becomes: field under the strip; before you type, `RECENT` (last six queries, mono timestamps) and `CONTINUE` (things you started and dropped). As you type, YOUR OWN LIBRARY ANSWERS FIRST under an `IN YOUR LIBRARY` slug with no spinner — local-first must be visible, not merely true. A hairline separates provider results under a `TMDB` slug, which carries an amber `SEARCHING` chip while in flight and, offline, the explicit line "Could not reach TMDB. Showing your library only." Three states, at last, in the place people hit them most.

4 · DISCOVER — now: four identical stacked cards each repeating "Because you watched Dark and Andor" verbatim, each with two competing pills (a pink Track and a grey Watchlist), and a 96×200 poster slot that is 90% empty.
  Becomes: the reason is promoted to a section head — `BECAUSE YOU WATCHED DARK` as a slug, once — with a horizontal shelf of title cards beneath it, then the next reason, then the next. One action per card, `+ WATCHLIST`; Track lives on the title screen where the season picker is. Add a left-swipe NOT FOR ME — there is currently no way to give the recommender a negative signal anywhere in the app, so it can only ever get louder about Dark. The TMDB attribution line stays, in `micro`, at the foot.

5 · YOU (was More) — now: a paragraph about local-first storage, a grey 3-up stat block, then seven identical chevron rows in which Account, Remote, Stats and Your data all look equally important.
  Becomes: handle → the ten-row strip (one year per row) → `264 WATCHES · 5.5 DAYS · 12 SHOWS` in figure-xl mono → Lists as poster stacks → People → and settings demoted to a plain `body-s` list on `surface` at the bottom with no chevrons-as-decoration. Stats folds in here; it should never have been a separate screen with its own header style. The local-first sentence becomes one `micro` line under the strip, because the strip already proves it.

## Motion

Motion in this direction has exactly one job: showing the record change. Nothing loops, nothing pulses, nothing breathes.

  STRIKE (the signature). Marking watched: the row lifts 2px, the poster's bottom rating bar fills left-to-right over 220ms, and a new bar strikes up from zero into the strip's empty right-hand slot over 180ms, cubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1). The strip shifts left by one bar width. Undo runs it backwards, which is what makes the existing `justMarked` / `undo` copy feel safe rather than merely available.
  BUILD. The strip renders bars staggered 8ms left-to-right on first paint of a screen — ~1.2s for 264 bars, capped at 600ms however long the record is.
  SCRUB. Thumb on the strip: the bloom card tracks under the finger with no easing (direct manipulation must be 1:1), snaps home in 160ms on release.
  MATCH. Tapping a poster carries that poster into the title screen header as a shared element, 260ms. This is the only place geometry travels between screens.
  PUSH. Screen transitions 240ms horizontal.

  REDUCED MOTION (AccessibilityInfo is already wired in `useReducedMotion`, so this is a styling decision, not new plumbing):
    strike → the bar is simply there, rating bar already full, 100ms cross-fade
    build → all bars at full height at once, no stagger
    scrub → unchanged; it is direct manipulation, not animation, and removing it would remove the feature
    match → cross-fade, no geometry
    push → cross-fade
    the loading sweep → a static striped fill; the sentence carries the state
  Reduced motion never removes information. It removes travel.

  PROGRESS. Wherever a count is known — 3 of 12 imports, 7 of 40 episodes matched — the indicator is DETERMINATE and amber, and the number is in mono beside it. An indeterminate sweep appears only where the total genuinely is not known, and it is a 1px line, not a spinner. This is the visual half of the "nothing / could not look / still working" rule the codebase already enforces in prose.

## Watching Mode

The flagship, and today it is the most compromised screen: a 15pt text clock, a five-chip control row the code's own comments admit measures 690px in a 390px viewport at 200% text, emoji-pad labels at 9pt (unreadable in a dark room, which is the only room this screen is used in), a pad only four rows tall before the search field steals the bottom, a drift banner written as a grey sentence, and a remote drawer with no visible affordance whatsoever.

THE HISTOGRAM IS THE SCREEN. Top 44pt: not a clock, a timeline of this episode. Full-width, `ink` with an `edge` hairline, runtime end to end, and drawn behind it in `faint` grey the reaction density of everyone who ever watched this episode — the punchline is a hill, the reveal is a spike, and you can see them coming. A 2px `here`-magenta playhead marks your position. `00:14:07 / 00:44:00` in Plex Mono 15 sits right-aligned above it.
  This makes drift VISIBLE instead of textual. The existing `estimateDriftMs` banner — "You look behind the crowd by 4s — tap to correct" — stays as the accessible path, but the primary gesture becomes dragging the playhead onto the hill. You can literally see you are early. That is the interaction people will describe.
  Your own reactions draw ON TOP in magenta as you make them. By the end of the episode you have drawn a second histogram over the crowd's.

THE STREAM. Four lanes as now, arriving on timestamp, fading left, `REACTION_VISIBLE_MS` unchanged. Reactions render as chips — emoji at 22 plus the reactor's initial in mono 11 — on a 60%-opacity `surface` plate with a 1px `edge`, so they stay legible over any still. Hold and density both survive untouched; they are correct and WCAG 2.2.2 requires the first. Under reduced motion, reactions APPEAR AND FADE IN PLACE at their lane position rather than drifting — the feature survives, the travel does not. The burst summariser stays exactly as written.

THE PAD. 5 across, filling everything below the stream — roughly 5 rows instead of the current 4. Emoji at 34, label in `micro` 11 on `surface`, 1px `edge` on press. Row one is PINNED and never reflows when you search: 👍 👎 😂 😭 😱 — the permanent fixtures the research asked for. Search moves from a full-width field at the bottom to a 44pt magnifier button in the control bar that expands over the pad, because the pad is the point and search is the fallback.

THE CONTROL BAR. The five wrapping chips collapse into one fixed bottom bar of 44pt targets: PAUSE · HOLD · −5s · +5s · ⋯ , where ⋯ opens a sheet holding density, ListenSync and the remote. At 200% it becomes two rows of 44pt by design instead of overflowing by accident.

THE REMOTE DRAWER gets an affordance: a 4×64pt magenta nub on the right edge, always visible, labelled "Open the TV remote". Inside, the mixed idioms go — no more orange system ⏪ emoji sitting next to hand-drawn vector triangles as in 14-remote — replaced by the same 1.5pt stroke set, with Play/Pause as one 88pt centre target. The device state line is honest as it already is, but the buttons are visibly disabled (`faint` strokes, `accessibilityState.disabled`) until a device is found, instead of looking pressable with nothing attached.

THE EXIT. Leaving Watching Mode shows what you made: "You reacted 14 times" over the episode histogram with your bars in magenta above the crowd's grey, and MARK WATCHED as the primary action. Your reactions become bars in the strip. The flagship feature feeds the record, which is the whole thesis.
  Watching Mode is dark-only regardless of theme, and says so in one `micro` line the first time a light-theme user opens it.

## What this direction refuses to do

- No badges, streaks, trophies, levels or year-in-review confetti. The strip growing by one bar is the entire reward system, and it is the honest one — it cannot be gamed, it does not nag, and it gets more valuable the longer you keep it. Doc 03 §2.2 records simplicity as the aggressively-stated top-line demand and names gamification as what made the alternatives feel clunky; doc 03 §5 already defers badges and year-in-review explicitly. This direction makes that deferral permanent by giving the reward job to the record itself.
- No glass, no blur, no gradient on any surface that carries data. Backgrounds are flat, separation is a 1px hairline. The only two gradients in the app are the rating ramp and the reaction histogram, and both encode a number you can read off. A blurred panel over a poster grid costs contrast on the exact pixels that hold the information, and this is a tracker whose users have libraries in the thousands.
- No skeleton shimmer, and no spinner standing in for a fact. A thing that is loading names what it is loading and shows how far it has got — '3 of 12', determinate, in amber. A thing that failed says it failed and offers the local answer, because the local answer always exists here. The app will never show a pulsing grey rectangle that could equally mean empty, loading or broken, which is precisely what the current 64x96 grey poster fallback means today.
